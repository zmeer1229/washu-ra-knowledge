Active Shooter/Violent Intruder

Monday, June 9, 2025

2:01 PM

Response actions (Remember ABC):  

Avoid:  

Evacuate the building immediately if it can be done in a safe manner.  

Do NOT carry any personal belongings with you and avoid elevators and escalators if possible.  

If you are located in a high-rise building and the shooter is below, ascend as many floors as possible. Once a safe area is reached, secure the location and move away from the entranceway to a more secure location. If the shooter is above you, move down and out of the building.   

When evacuating in the stairwell, stay pressed to the wall to allow responding officer’s room to ascend quickly and safely.   

Once outside the building, run toward law enforcement with your hands visible and raised above your head.  

Barricade:  

If it is possible to do safely, move to a central and secure area of the building. If able, lock or barricade the door. You can block the door using whatever is available: desks, tables, file cabinets, other furniture, books, etc. Turn off lights.  

Block the door with large heavy objects to make entry as difficult as possible (desks, tables, file cabinets, furniture, books, etc.). If the only means available to barricade the door is with your body, attempt to stay lower than average waist level to avoid any shots fired through the door by the shooter.  

After securing the room, people should be positioned out of sight and behind items that might offer additional protection - wall, desks, file cabinets, bookshelves, etc.   

Remain quiet. Do not speak on the phone. Only use text messages if you need to communicate with others. Remember, a phone ringing is only ringing, but talking is different.  

Confront:  

There is no single procedure that can be recommended in this situation.   

If hiding or flight is impossible, remain quiet or "play dead" to avoid detection.   

Last resort options if you come face to face with the assailant are twofold:  

Attempt to quickly overpower the individual with force in the most violent manner possible.   

If you are with other people you should work as a collective group to overcome the shooter.   

Remember, the attacker will continue to shoot victims unless they are stopped.  

Un-Securing an area:  

Responding police or emergency dispatch units will assist in un-securing and rescuing victims who are locked down. Do not engage in un-securing rooms unless directed to do so.   

Remember, the shooter will not stop until they are engaged by an outside force.   

Attempts to rescue people should only be attempted if it can be accomplished without further endangering the persons inside a secured area.   

If doubt exists for the safety of the individuals inside the room, the area should remain secured  

REPORT  

Call WUPD and give them the following information as calmly as possible:  

Your name.  

Location of the incident (provide as many specific details as possible).   

Your exact location.  

Number of shooters, the location at which they were last seen, and the direction in which they traveled   

Physical description of the shooter (sex, race, clothing, type of weapons).  

Articulate the number and location of victims and provide a brief description of injuries   

If you have heard explosions in addition to gunshots.  

If you observed any suspicious devices (improvised explosive devices), provide a description and the location at which it was seen.   

Do not hang up unless the WUPD operator tells you to. They may want you to remain on the phone to help target where the shooter is.  

***The protocol below should be followed once the threat has been contained or neutralized***  

Upon lockdown being lifted, seek medical attention if needed.  Your safety and care is what’s most important.  

Contact WUPD and RCD On-Call and go to location directed to by WUPD or RCD (i.e., some cases could be your Emergency Assembly Point (EAP) and other cases could be told to remain in place).  

Send students to the EAP as directed by Residential Life leadership.  

Remain calm and encourage residents to stay calm.  Stay visible to your residents.   

Gather information about the well-being of the residents.    

Document the incident in Advocate immediately, including all pertinent and important information.  If you don’t have access to Advocate, document the incident in a word document or email and send to the RCD On-Call.
